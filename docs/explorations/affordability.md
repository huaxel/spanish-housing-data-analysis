# Affordability: years of net household income for 90 m²

`afford_90m2_years = eur_m2_libre * 90 / renta_hogar_neta`, computed in the
CCAA mart (income year = ECV survey year − 1; see methods §1–2). 90 m² is an
explicit reference size, not a claim about what households buy. Appraisal
levels × survey incomes: both are means, so this is a *representative*
ratio, blind to within-region inequality — the ADRH atlas (municipal) and
ECV deciles are the queued microscope.

## Nacional: prices at records, affordability not at its worst

| año | €/m² libre | renta neta/hogar | 90 m² en años de renta |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2008 | 2,071 | 30,045 | **6.20** |
| 2013 | 1,495 | 26,154 | 5.15 |
| 2019 | 1,641 | 30,690 | 4.81 |
| 2024 | 1,914 | 38,994 | **4.42** |

The bust's price crash (−28% €/m²) bought only one year of affordability
(6.2→5.2), because incomes crashed alongside. The recovery's price boom was
outrun by incomes (+30% renta 2008–24 vs −8% €/m²): 2024 national
affordability is the best in the series *despite record prices*.

## 2024 cross-section: two Spains

Worst: Balears (6.41), Madrid (6.15), País Vasco (5.03), Cataluña (4.73),
Canarias (4.45). These combine high €/m² with incomes that don't compensate —
Madrid's renta (€47k, highest) still can't cover €3,686/m².

Memory from 2013: País Vasco (6.58) and Balears (6.21) were *less* affordable
at the trough than Madrid is today — the crash didn't rescue high-income,
supply-tight markets; it crushed low-price ones into brief affordability
(Extremadura 2013: ~3 years) while incomes fell.

## Limits

- Means-on-means: Madrid's 6.15 hides a renter at half the mean income.
- 90 m² uniform: new builds skew smaller in tight markets, larger where land
  is cheap — the reference flatters tight markets slightly.
- Appraisal (not transaction) levels × ECV (not tax-data) incomes: both are
  the best public series at CCAA grain, neither is ground truth for a buyer.
- No provincial incomes exist in Tempus (ECV is CCAA-only), so the
  provincia mart carries no affordability column — a documented grain limit.
